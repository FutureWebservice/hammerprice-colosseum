## 5. Bids

5.1 A bid is a declaration signed by you with your wallet by which you bindingly offer the seller to buy the called lot at the stated price in USDC. A bid is not a blockchain transaction, transfers nothing and costs nothing.

5.3 A bid is valid if (a) the signature is correct and covers this room, lot, amount and time, (b) the lot is open, (c) the bid meets the minimum increment above the highest bid, (d) you are not blocked, (e) you have registered a paddle for this room and (f) at the moment of the bid your wallet holds at least the bid amount in USDC, counting your other leading bids and sales still to be settled ("funded paddle"). We read your balance from the blockchain. If we cannot read it, the bid is refused. Your USDC stay in your wallet. We neither reserve nor lock them, so you could move them after a bid. 5.6 A room extends a lot if a valid bid arrives shortly before it closes. By default a bid in the last 15 seconds moves the closing time to 15 seconds after the bid, by at most 120 seconds in total per lot. Each room displays the rules that apply to it.

5.7 The seller may set a reserve price. The seller need not sell the lot below that price. If the highest valid bid is below the reserve, the lot closes without a sale.

5.8 Bids are visible to all participants in the room under the bidder's paddle number. The signed bids of a closed lot can be viewed and checked by anyone. They contain the bidder's wallet address.

5.9 The seller can withdraw a lot only before the first valid bid.

## 6. Hammer and conclusion of contract

6.1 By placing a lot in a room, the seller declares bindingly that they sell the lot to the bidder who, when the lot closes, has placed the highest valid bid, provided it meets the reserve price. 6.2 The "hammer" is the moment at which the closing time of the lot passes and the system determines the highest valid bid. Nobody decides at that moment. 6.3 We store the lot, the bids, the hammer and the rules applicable at the time. You can view this information via your wallet address or request it from us. ## 7. Settlement

7.1 After the hammer, the sale is settled in one blockchain transaction: the USDC go from the buyer to the seller, the fee to our fee address and any creator royalty to its recipient, and the token from the seller to the buyer. Either everything happens or nothing. There is no partial execution.

7.2 The transaction needs the signatures of the buyer and of the seller. Each of you signs the same transaction with your own wallet, in any order. The buyer's signature releases only the buyer's USDC, the seller's signature only the seller's token, and the transaction does nothing unless both are present. Neither key can move the card or the money alone. Before you sign, the Platform shows you the legs of the transaction and checks them against the agreed amounts.

7.3 Our server adds one further signature, that of our settlement authority, and sends the signed transaction to the network. That signature pays the network fees and account rent. It has no authority over any asset of the buyer or the seller and cannot move a card or money. We hold no key that can.

7.4 Each attempt is a signing round of about one minute. If both signatures are not present by the end of a round, the stored signatures are discarded and a new round can be started. Both parties must sign within the settlement window after the hammer. It is {{SETTLEMENT_WINDOW_MINUTES}} minutes by default, and the Platform shows it for every sale. The buyer must hold enough USDC. The seller must be online during the window and may not transfer, freeze, encumber, redeem from the vault or destroy the lot until settlement.

7.5 When the transaction is confirmed on the blockchain, possession of and risk in the token pass to the buyer. Both parties can see the status in the Platform and in any blockchain explorer.

7.6 If the transaction fails for technical reasons (for example network outage, error in a third-party program), nothing is transferred and the parties can try again within the window. If the window ends without a confirmed transaction, the settlement expires. The card and the money have not moved, the lot is shown as "not completed", and section 8 applies to the party who did not act. You have no claim to a new listing.

7.7 The physical card stays in the third party's vault. Whoever holds the token may have it redeemed under the vault operator's terms, which may incur costs and may destroy the token. We are not responsible for this.

## 22. Packs

22.1 A pack is a sealed draw from a pool of real cards that a pack operator owns. There are two kinds. In a chance pack the cards differ in rarity and in listed value; in an equal-value pack every card has the same listed value. Both are offered by independent pack operators, who are the sellers. Hammerprice provides the technology and the verification, is not the seller and does not receive the price. In demonstration mode Hammerprice itself operates one demo chance pack with replica cards without value and test USDC; it is marked as a demo and exists on the test network only.

22.2 Before you buy, the Platform shows the price, the operator, the odds per rarity and the full pool. They are fixed by a hash before the first sale, and the operator cannot change the pool after sales start. If a rarity is sold out, its share is spread over the others in proportion. The listed value of a card is the operator's own figure and not a promise of a price.

22.3 Which card you get is decided by a random draw (ECVRF) with a public proof. In a chance pack the draw is made only after your payment is final on the blockchain: no card is chosen or shown before that, and the input of the draw contains the signature of your payment and a block that was produced after it, so neither you nor we can steer it. In an equal-value pack the card is drawn when you start the purchase. On the proof page anyone can recompute the draw from the pool, your seed and the beacon. You do not know in advance which card you will get.

22.4 Chance pack: you pay first, and you pay the pack operator directly. Your USDC go to the operator's wallet, with the fee under section 9 as a separate part of the same payment, in a transaction that you sign and for which our settlement authority only pays the network fees. We never receive the price. After your payment is final, the card is drawn. The operator must then deliver it to your wallet in a second transaction that the operator signs, within the delivery deadline (24 hours after the draw unless set otherwise); our settlement authority again only pays the network fees. Because we hold no money, we cannot refund you. If the operator does not deliver in time, or moves the drawn card away, the sale is marked as not delivered, the operator receives a strike (section 8) and the pack is paused, and no second draw is made. The pack page then shows you the operator's wallet, the signature of your payment and the proof of the draw, so that you can claim against the operator yourself. A card that the operator still delivers late reaches your wallet. The delivery record of the operator (delivered, late and not delivered sales and the median delivery time) is public and is shown before you pay, together with a notice of this risk. The state of your purchase (pay, confirming, drawing, waiting for the operator, delivered or not delivered) is shown on the pack page. If you do not complete the payment within the time shown, nothing is charged. Equal-value pack: you pay in the same kind of single transaction as in section 7: your USDC go to the operator, less the fee under section 9, and the card moves from the operator's wallet to yours. You and the operator both sign. Hammerprice holds neither your money nor the cards. If the signing round runs out before everything is signed, nothing is charged and you can try again.

22.5 You must be 18 or older and you confirm it before every purchase. There is a daily limit per wallet, set per pack (5 unless the pack says otherwise). There is no balance and no credit: nothing is stored for later, and a purchase is a payment to the operator and a card, nothing else. The daily limit counts a purchase from the moment you start it and gives it back when the purchase is never paid or fails. A pack operator cannot buy their own pack.
